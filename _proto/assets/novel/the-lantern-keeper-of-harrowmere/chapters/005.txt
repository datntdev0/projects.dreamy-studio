The glassmaker's shop on Cooper Lane was the only one in Harrowmere with its shutters open. Inside, an old man named Ostrel Fenn was grinding a lens by candlelight.
"Lamp glass," he said, without looking up. "She breaks one every winter. The wind finds the old panes and the cold does the rest."
Maro asked who paid for the glass. Ostrel laughed, a dry sound like sand in a bottle. "Nobody pays. I make it, she carries it up. That is the arrangement."
On the shelf behind him stood a row of small green bottles, each stoppered with wax. Maro asked what was in them. "Salt water," said Ostrel. "From the Quay, at the turn of the tide. Do not ask me why."
