The bell hung beside the lamp for three days and did not ring. On the fourth night the wind rose, and every other sound in the tower stopped to listen.
Wren sat on the top step with her back against the wall. She had pulled the rope tight and tied it off with her father's knot, and now she watched the clapper, which hung perfectly still in the gale.
Maro climbed up at midnight, out of breath, with his coat buttoned to the chin. "Does it always do that?" he asked.
"It has never done anything," said Wren. "That is what worries me."
Below them, very faintly, something answered. It was not a bell. It was closer to a breath drawn through water.
