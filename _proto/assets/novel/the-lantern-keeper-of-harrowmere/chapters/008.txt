The salt merchant's debt was older than the Council. It had been written into the founding charter of Harrowmere, in a clause nobody read: the town owed the sea one lamp, lit every night, for as long as the harbor stood.
Maro found the clause in the third volume of the charter, folded behind a map. The ink had gone brown.
He read it twice, then a third time, and then he laughed, quietly, because it was the first sensible thing he had found in Harrowmere.
It did not say what happened to the town if the lamp went dark. It said only that the sea would collect.
