At low water the next night, Wren took a rowing boat out over the Drowned Quay. Maro sat in the stern with the ledger on his knees and the lantern between his feet.
The water was perfectly clear. Below the keel the old harbor lay spread out like a map: the quay stones, the broken piers, and the roofs of the lower town, which had sunk before the fog came.
A single window glowed on one of those roofs, a pale green square, steady as a pilot light.
"I thought you were lighting it for the ships," said Maro.
"I was never lighting it for the ships," said Wren. She shipped the oars and let the boat drift. "I was lighting it for them."
