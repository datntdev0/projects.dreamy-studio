Nobody in Vitra remembered the sea as water. To the city it was the Glass, a flat bright plain of fused sand that began at the foot of the lower districts and ran to the horizon.
Ines Marlowe walked out onto it every morning before the shift bells, in boots with soles of salvaged tire. The Glass was warm under her feet, as it always was, and it hummed faintly if she stood still long enough.
She had been a salvager for nine years. In that time she had pulled cables, hinges and one complete bathtub out of the glass, and none of it had ever hummed back.
This morning something under her boots did.
