The object was the size of a loaf of bread, black and lightly ribbed, set into the glass as if the sea had hardened around it. Ines cleared the surface with a brush and found a seam, then a second seam, then a row of tiny sockets.
It was not salvage. Nothing in the old registry looked like it. She photographed it from four sides and told no one.
At the shift bell she walked back to the lower districts with her collar up, past the Ration Office and the stacked cisterns, and let herself into the room she rented above a cooper's yard.
She set the photographs on the table and sat looking at them until the lamp burned down. In the last photograph, the object's sockets were glowing.
