The tide tables of Harrowmere had been drawn by hand for two hundred years, and for two hundred years they had been correct. Then, seven winters ago, they began to drift.
Maro sat up all night with the parchment chart from the glassmaker's shop and a candle stub, working the numbers. The error was small, a few minutes each tide. It grew in a steady curve.
By dawn he had the answer. The tide was not failing. It was running late because something was holding the water back, as a hand holds a door.
He wrote the figure at the top of a clean page and underlined it twice. The number was three hundred and twelve.
